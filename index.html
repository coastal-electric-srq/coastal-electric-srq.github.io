Dedicated circuits

A dedicated circuit runs from its own breaker to one piece of equipment, with nothing else on it. We add them for pool heat pumps, HVAC heat pumps, EV chargers, hot tubs, and large appliances like ranges and dryers. We size the breaker and wire from the unit's nameplate and install instructions, and check that your panel has room first. Text a photo of the nameplate and your panel to (941) 500-5019.

We also replace mobile home and RV pedestals and track down outlets that stopped working, single breakers that trip and flickering lights.

Questions about hiring an electrician in Sarasota

How do I check that an electrician is licensed in Florida?

Search the contractor's name or license number on the Florida DBPR license search at myfloridalicense.com. Ours is State of Florida Certified Electrical Contractor (License EC13012644).

What's the difference between a certified and a registered electrical contractor?

In Florida, a certified electrical contractor holds a certificate of competency from the state. A registered electrical contractor may contract only in the local jurisdiction where the registration was issued (Fla. Stat. 489.505). We're state certified.

Do you do residential electrical work?

Yes. Most of our work is in homes and condos: pool and spa electrical, pool and HVAC heat pump circuits, AC disconnects, dedicated circuits, panels, EV chargers, interlocks, surge protection and lighting.

Should I call an electrician or my HVAC or pool company?

For a rusted AC disconnect, a tripping pool pump breaker or a heat pump that won't power on, an electrician checks the circuit, breaker and disconnect. Your HVAC or pool company handles the equipment itself. Don't open energized equipment; text us photos instead.

Do you handle permits?

We handle the permit with the building department that covers your address when the job needs one.

What is a dedicated circuit and when do you need one?

A dedicated circuit runs from its own breaker to a single piece of equipment, with nothing else on it. The code calls it an individual branch circuit (NEC Article 100). You need one when the equipment's install manual or nameplate calls for it, which is common for pool heat pumps, HVAC heat pumps, EV chargers (NEC 625.40), hot tubs, ranges and dryers, or when the load is too big to share a circuit (NEC 210.23). Text us a photo of the nameplate and your panel.
